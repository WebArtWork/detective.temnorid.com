About

Four sacks of grain are missing from the granary of Greyford, and the night watchman is dead. Walk the village, read what was left behind, hear the people out, compare their accounts, and tell the elder whom you believe, or that we do not know. About 15–25 minutes, seen through classic fixed cameras. The text of the game is in Ukrainian.

The village elder has accusations but no proof. You have a few hours of free exploration: four places, ten things to find, five people to talk to. Nobody is a cartoon villain, and everybody has a reason not to tell you everything. When you are ready you submit a report, and the village acts on it.

Nothing in the game ever tells you whether your report was right: no green tick, no red cross, no percentages. A wrong report is a normal ending with consequences, and so is deciding there is not enough evidence. The graphics are simple shapes for now, and there is no magic or creature in this case.

Features

Investigate

Four connected places and ten clues: observations, records and traces. Read them again whenever you like; nothing is used up.

Interview

Five villagers with fixed, honest-to-the-story answers. Ask again after you find something new and some of them say more.

Decide

A case board with evidence, people, a timeline that tells observed from reported, and a decision tab. Accuse a suspect, or report insufficient evidence.

  • Fixed cinematic cameras in every part of the village, with smooth cuts and controls that never turn around on you.
  • Discrepancies are only marked as "accounts differ": you decide what they mean.
  • Autosave, Continue, and a fresh case on every replay.
  • Play alone, or share the village with a second investigator (one hosts, one joins): a shared notebook, and the host files the report.
  • Touch controls for phones, and a Windows game that updates itself.
  • A sign-in screen like the one in Lords (Google or guest), which you can skip: the whole case plays without an account.

Case 01: The Missing Grain

Greyford, one night. The granary is short of four sacks and its watchman did not see the morning. Start at the granary, and go wherever the trail takes you.

System requirements and limitations

Windows 10 or newer, 64-bit, a graphics card that runs Direct3D 11. The game is light; it has not been measured on a range of machines, so no frame-rate promises are made. This is an early-access version: one case, Ukrainian text only, no voice, no gamepad.

Playing with others

Two investigators can share one village. Choose Play with others in the main menu: one player hosts, the other joins with the address it shows or an online code. Each of you walks with your own fixed camera and sees the other in the village. The host owns the case: clues, statements, discrepancies, pins and the report. What you inspect or ask is checked by the host and then shows in both notebooks. The guest can lean toward a choice on the decision board, but only the host files the report, and the ending is shown to both. Online cases are not saved, so your own saved case is never touched. If the guest leaves, the host goes on alone; if the host leaves, the guest returns to the menu.

Phones

Android and iOS builds are made from Unity Build Profiles. On a phone there is a thumb stick, an Action button (inspect or talk), a Run button and a pause button, and the journal and case board are tabs on the bottom bar. They have not been tried on a device yet, and no phone build is published.

Updates

The Windows game updates itself from the newest GitHub release when it starts. You can turn this off in Profile.

Part of the world of Temnorid

The first time you press Play, a sign-in screen like the one in Lords offers Sign in with Google (on Windows, when it is set up on the device), Play as a guest or Play offline. You can sign in later from Profile, and a signed-in player starts with their Google name. The case plays the same with or without an account, and offline.

Detective does not read or write anything in Lords: the Lords incident in this version is a local stand-in, and your report changes nothing in Lords.
